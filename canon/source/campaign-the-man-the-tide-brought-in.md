# The Man the Tide Brought In

*A campaign for Ovara in 18 chapters, 9 sessions*

Places, creatures, the gift and the craft are all defined in the world bible. This document uses its terms: **reach** (1 to 5), **ebbed**, **the undertow**, **ordering**, **fouled**, **deaf** and **orphan water**, **chorus** and **shared calling**.

---

## How to use this

- **Length.** Two chapters per session, nine sessions. Chapters 1 to 4 are the introduction and end with Jef's death. For a shorter run, merge chapters 8 and 9 into one evening ashore and play chapter 16 as a montage.
- **The hero.** The campaign follows one character: a man with no memory, called Wrack, later revealed as Ilan Voss. He is written to be a player character.
- **The villain.** One man stands behind everything the hero fights: Anselm Rook, chairman of Meridian Assurance. He is described below. He appears in person in chapters 4, 6, 10 and 17.
- **More players.** Others take the companions below. Each has their own stake and a chapter where they matter most.
- **Each chapter lists** where it happens, what is new, the challenge, the powers in play, how the hero grows stronger, and how it ends. A final line, **Scenes and texture**, lists the quieter moments the novel uses to introduce people and places. Use them to slow the pace between set pieces.
- **The route** runs the length of the world: Anthem Waters, the Pale, Anthem Waters again, the Quiet, the Girdle, the Anthem Waters, the Bright Shelves, the Girdle. No region is visited the same way twice.
- **The hero fails three times** (chapters 5, 11 and 15). Each failure costs something that is not given back cheaply. Do not soften them.

### What the game master knows from the start

Wrack is **Ilan Voss**, Chief Surveyor of Halcyon Dredge & Mineral, and he is Shallowborn. His father was an agravar dock engineer in Saltgate; his mother was a mariner who hid ashore and taught him to hide too. At sixteen, a week after his father's funeral, he won the Meridian Scholarship: a short grey rod of Maker work flickered in his hands. From then on Anselm Rook paid for him, placed him at Keelhaven and then at Halcyon, and dined with him once a year for twenty-two years. He was the nearest thing to a father Ilan had.

Ilan planned Halcyon's dumping routes, including the one over Low Anthem. At Rook's quiet request he led the dig at Site Nine, where the drowned Maker city woke at his touch. When he rang Rook to say that the Makers were one people and that he meant to tell the islands, Rook sent Adler Crane, who threw him overboard with his wrists tied.

Sinking, he let go of everything at once, and the **undertow** took his memory. The sea kept it. The rays have it.

He is the first person ever to hold both the craft and the gift, and his reach is 5, which nobody has had before. Nobody knows why. Mariners will come to say the sea chose someone who belonged to both peoples.

### The villain: Anselm Rook

Full background is in the world bible, section 13 (secrets 8 to 10). In play:

- **What he is.** An agravar of about seventy-two, Jef's fifty-ninth apprentice, Threaded for forty-nine years, with no gift and hands that cannot open Maker work. Maelis shared the Hush with him for a year when both were young.
- **What he wants.** Ground for forty million islanders, made by running the Makers' engine in the drowned city with the Dragon's heart to drive it. He accepts that the sea around it will die and has tanks ready for the mariners.
- **What he needs.** Ilan's hands, to seat the engine, and the Dragon's heart. He cannot mend the Dragon, so he lets the hero do it (chapter 6) and waits for it to come where he can reach it (chapters 14 and 15).
- **How he works.** Through paper, other people and patience. He never raises his voice, never lies about what he has done, and never does a thing with his own hands. He would much rather have Ilan back than beat him.
- **His tools.** Meridian's debts; the white refuge ships; a third of the Threaded; Corin; the two Maker darts of Jef's making that Jef never found; and the heads of the other boards, who all owe him.

## Session 1: Washed up

### Chapter 1. Wrack

- **Where:** Low Anthem, in the Anthem Waters. Green-gold light, kelp sixty metres tall humming in the current, tide hounds nosing at the stranger.
- **New:** mariner life: a small poor hold, its terraces, its elders, its half-dark dome.
- **Opening:** he wakes on the seabed breathing water, in surface boots and a torn grey jacket with a ripped corporate crest, cable-tie marks on his wrists. Sefa finds him and names him Wrack, the word for what the tide brings in.
- **Challenge:** the elders must decide whether he is a mariner, a spy or bait. He has to earn a place with no past to offer: work the terraces, learn the songs, and sit through a healer's examination that finds his memory is not damaged but taken, the mark of the undertow.
- **Powers in play:** Sefa's small healing; the hold's rituals of tending machines nobody understands.
- **Grows stronger:** passing the meeting-hall lamp, dark for three generations, his hands open it and mend it. Nobody but Jef has done that in six hundred years.
- **Ends with:** the lamp lit, the hall silent, and an elder saying quietly that someone must send word north.
- **Scenes and texture:** the swim in through tended kelp to the half-dark dome; the sea-door; three days in a dry room with Sefa as self-appointed guard, explaining holds, Makers and the gift; Elder Oro, who speaks last; Davin showing him the gate latch four times; the day-song; lampweed on the dark side; the night crew's hammock talk of Jef, the Dragon and the Threaded.
- **Seed:** in the hammock talk, a boy says that one of the Threaded once went up to the islands and never came down.

### Chapter 2. The barge

- **Where:** the terraces of Low Anthem at night, under a Halcyon dumping route.
- **Before the barge:** word of the lamp goes north by long-call. People begin leaving him gifts. With Elder Oro he tries the eleven dead lamps: one more lights, one is broken beyond what his bare hands can do, nine will not open. Davin explains Tenders and Breakers at the net-racks.
- **New:** what the agravars do to the sea. Tenders and Breakers. Tidecalling, shown by Hesper, who on her best day can stop a fishing boat.
- **Challenge:** a waste barge dumps over the terraces. The Tenders net and seal and plant bitterwrack as always. Wrack's anger answers instead. The sea lifts the barge clear, pours the sludge back into it and sets it down a kilometre off. It is a Raise far beyond any Mighty, in fouled water, and he cannot control it: the night crew is nearly drowned.
- **Powers in play:** Raise at reach 5, untrained. He is ebbed for three days afterwards, cold and shaking. Hesper teaches him the first rule: *ask, do not order.* What he did was half an order and half an apology, which is why the sea did it badly.
- **The cost:** the barge crew reports it. Halcyon gunboats drop charges on Low Anthem "in response to an attack on shipping". Part of the dome floods. Twenty-one people die, Elder Oro and Davin among them, and it is his fault. The dead are laid at the kelp stems.
- **Ends with:** Hesper, burying her dead, leaving for the Breakers in the Tangle. Okonta arriving on a silver needle: she felt the barge rise from eighty kilometres away, and she is taking him to Jef.
- **Behind the scenes:** a deckhand's drawing of the barge on its pillar of sea reaches Rook's desk within three weeks. He knows whose hands they are.
- **Scenes and texture:** Ione's forearm splashed in the fall, which explains her scars; Hesper's lesson on the floor of the hall (contact, one purpose, standing); Sefa at the terrace edge telling him to come back because he owes them a dome.

## Session 2: The old man

### Chapter 3. The Dragon

- **Where:** the Tangle, the edge of the Slick, and Greywatch Stone.
- **New:** wild sea and its dangers. The first agravars seen up close: a recovery team and the wreckers they hired. The fouled.
- **Challenge:** three days' run to the calling stone with hunters behind, and three days' wait there. They cut through the Tangle, dark and full of wrecks, and are driven to the edge of the Slick. There, in deaf water, the gift does not answer at all, and slick eels hunt by taste. Wrack is helpless; Okonta's flying gets them out. At Greywatch Stone the Dragon is days late and the hunters catch up. Their officer looks at Wrack, goes pale, and calls him "sir" before she remembers her orders: *he wants him breathing.*
- **Powers in play:** the Thread and a needle at full speed; deaf water, learned the hard way; pale singers heard far off, which Okonta steers by.
- **Grows stronger:** when the Dragon comes it stops for him, which it has never done for anyone. Through his palm he feels what is wrong with it, and with the gift driving water through its ribs and his hands in its workings he mends it enough to run, at well under its proper pace. Gift and craft together for the first time.
- **Ends with:** three days north in its belly, and one question: why did she call him sir?
- **Scenes and texture:** the first night's camp, pale singers heard far off, and Okonta's own story and the rule of the Threaded; the ruin of Sounding in the Tangle, with her warning not to raise it with Jef; at Greywatch Stone, a woman of Fallow waiting with her dying father and Brask of Rime sharing smoked fish. The Dragon carries all of them. The three days north are a journey to play out: the gallery and its names, dark holds seen from above, pale singers pacing the Dragon by night, the underside of the ice, Rime hanging amber beneath it, and stepping off at walking pace at the lip of the Well.
- **Seed:** in the row of cradles, one near the end stands empty, with silt in it and a short square island name cut beside it that somebody once tried to scratch out.

### Chapter 4. The mechanic

- **Where:** the Pale. Kettle House on the lip of the north Well; the Lantern far below, a single star in black water; lantern jellies drifting past the window.
- **New:** Jef, Maelis and the great rays. The Wells, where the gift is a reach stronger. The well-wardens, who let them down when Jef shows his token.
- **Challenge:** the house is empty; they wait, fed by ice-divers from Rime. When Jef and Maelis arrive, Jef is not a god but a tired mechanic with no gift at all and perhaps a season left. He tries to teach nine hundred years in it. Each lesson is a trial: hold the water of three hundred healing pools still for six days while Jef mends the Lantern's heart, then help him while still holding; hold a sphere of sea in the air with a living shoal inside. All nine halls of the Lantern relight.
- **Powers in play:** all five forms of calling, practised one by one until he can ask instead of shout. The craft, from Tend to Mend.
- **The visitor:** in the seventh week an old-pattern needle comes up at the landing. Its rider is an islander of seventy with Thread scars on his neck. Jef calls him Anselm. Maelis bars her kitchen to him, so he is given soup on the step. He says he has found the city under the Girdle and asks Jef to come and seat its engine, or to say how. Jef refuses. The visitor then offers Wrack his name, his past and his worth if he will come south. Wrack asks who tied his hands; the visitor says only that he knows. Wrack stays. Jef will not say who the man is: *you'd have had your whole life handed to you by that man, in his voice, with his price on it.*
- **Grows stronger:** control. Jef gives him his worn hand tool. Then, with what he has left, Jef lays one final Thread. He does not ask Wrack to swear the rule.
- **The death:** two nights after the Thread, a Maker dart with no rider comes under the ice, driven from an old-pattern hull at the ice edge. It goes down the Well through the wardens, killing two, and strikes the Lantern's heart. It is one of the forty Jef built at nineteen; he broke thirty-eight and never found the other two. Jef wakes, puts on his apron and goes down. Wrack holds three hundred pools for the nine-hour descent and the eleven hours after. Jef borrows his tool back, mends the heart from the crawlway and dies under it, with the tool held out handle first. Eight halls relight; the ninth is cracked and stays dark.
- **The instruction:** on the way down he tells Maelis: *he has to ask for his memory himself.* Not be told it, by Anselm or by her.
- **Ends with:** Maelis taking Jef south on the Dragon to Ember House, with the rays in escort. She says the visitor is "a boy I grew up with" and that she will tell the rest at the Hush, because it is the same story. Far out over the Well, the Dragon's light flickers. **The introduction ends here.**
- **Scenes and texture:** eleven days waiting on the step, fed by Brask and his cousins, learning to Listen at the lip of the Well; soup on the first night and Jef examining his hands; Jef letting his history fall in pieces on the step while saying nothing of Sounding, and saying of his sixty apprentices that one carried off everything but the hands; Maelis telling how the rays chose her and how she came to marry him; the rays standing still in the Well when the visitor comes; the dinner Jef cooks before the Thread, with Okonta describing what it is like to carry it; Maelis's argument behind the still-room door; the afternoon on the lip of the Well when Jef says *teach everybody, teach people you don't like* and *it doesn't get lighter for being left*; Maelis tying his apron; Rime and the healers lining the shelf when he is carried to the Dragon.

## Session 3: The dark

### Chapter 5. The lamps go out

- **Where:** Deep Chorus, the canyon hold, where every voice echoes.
- **New:** the world without Jef. The elders' secret comes into the open as the oldest holds go dark. Lampweed hung in the halls, the sign of a hold in trouble. Meridian's white ships.
- **Challenge:** word comes that the Dragon has stopped. On the way to it he finds Deep Chorus with its air-hearts failing, twelve thousand people, and elders who will not admit it. Three of the seven hearts are silent and a fourth is dying. He must choose which to mend first, hold or Dragon, and whichever he chooses, the other gets worse.
- **Powers in play:** the craft at a scale he has never tried. He buys time by holding air in three thousand rooms by Shape and Hold, and risks the undertow a second time.
- **The failure:** he mends one dead heart and saves the dying one. The other two are cracked through at the hinge. They want making, and he cannot make. Light returns only as far as the sixtieth tier. The forty tiers below are sealed for good.
- **The cost:** the undertow takes the tending song and the name of Sefa's hound before she slaps him out of it. It does not give them back.
- **Sefa's moment:** she walks into the elders' high hall, which carries every word to every gallery, and tells the whole hold the truth. The forty choirs organise the move upward; Aurel, the hold's Mighty, holds one small bubble over the infirmary in shifts.
- **Rook's hand:** Elder Vey admits that a man in grey came to her sea-door in a glass bell and offered the whole hold a refuge of tanks and lamps for twenty years' service each. She sent him away. Three holds to the north did not.
- **Ends with:** Vey saying the Dragon will not run again, and thirty small pale lights moving along its length that were not there five days ago.
- **Scenes and texture:** nine silent days at Kettle House with Jef's mug still on the bench; the Threaded asking who they answer to now; Okonta's enquiries about a Threaded called Anselm; learning to ride a needle on the way south; the descent through the singing canyon to the tiers lit only by lampweed; Elder Vey's cold reception of Okonta, a daughter of the hold; the refugees of Low Anthem on the lowest tier; Vey at the sea-door afterwards.

### Chapter 6. The eldest brother

- **Where:** the gallery of the stalled Dragon, above Chorus Canyon; then the Slick.
- **New:** the Threaded as people, forty-one of them, gathered in the only house they have. Rook among them. Fighting between Threaded.
- **The news:** four more holds dark; refugees; Breakers sinking ships with crews aboard under a new caller from a bombed hold; Halcyon and Meridian "protective patrols"; white ships over the dark holds. Corin's hold, Kelder, has gone up in nets with his sister in it.
- **Rook's offer:** he arrives in the empty cradle that bears his name, in a hard suit and glass helmet. He says the rule was for a world with Jef in it. He offers the Threaded work as pilots and guides for the refuges, paid in deeded ground, with their own kin housed first. He will neither admit nor deny the dart. Privately he tells Wrack: *I aimed at a machine. He chose to go down to it.* And: *mend this one, if you can. I should like to see it run.* Fourteen needles leave after him.
- **Challenge:** that night Corin, Tam and a third come for Wrack while he lies in the Dragon's heart. He is far stronger and cannot finish a thought before a needle crosses the hall. One blind shove of water tears down six hammocks with sleepers in them; he flinches, and they net him in wrecker's steel and tow him into the Slick, where he cannot ask for anything.
- **The rescue:** Okonta follows alone and throws a torn pouch of dried fish across their path. Slick eels take Tam. Corin cuts the tow-line without hesitating and goes down after the boy. Okonta takes the net and a gash from wrist to elbow. *It was said to a dead man. — I said it. I wasn't dead.*
- **Grows stronger:** Okonta teaches him what she has seen the Mighty do. A fight is over before you have finished saying please, so the asking is done beforehand: Hold an arm's length of sea about you, already listening, and lean on it when something comes. She drills him with the needle's prow for days. Then, alone, he rebuilds the Dragon's heart to run on two wheels instead of three; the run from the canyon to the south Tip now takes four days instead of three, and a pole-to-pole run more than six. The spare wheel is lashed to a rib.
- **Ends with:** nineteen needles left in a row of cradles built for two hundred; the young man from Fallow going home to keep his own water; Sefa joining the journey with Elder Vey's blessing; and an unanswered question. Why would a man who owns the islands want the Dragon mended?
- **Scenes and texture:** the old woman with the clay tube, the brothers from Pearl Stair, the girl with the harpoon; the argument about what the rule is for; the sound of needles leaving their cradles, like knuckles cracking; Sefa stitching Okonta's arm, her first patient who is not from Low Anthem; twenty-seven Threaded standing round the dismantled heart as at a graveside.

## Session 4: The door and the port

### Chapter 7. The door

- **Where:** the Quiet. A steaming surface, the Smokers and their red tube-gardens, the Wheel, the black unlit Hush, and Ember House on its hot-spring islet.
- **New:** the rays as a people. Their gift is memory, and they keep what the undertow takes.
- **Challenge:** the rays will not let him reach Maelis until they have weighed him. It is a trial without words in the dark of the Wheel: they show him the sea as it was, and the sea as it is. Then Maelis, in mourning, offers a potion and asks what she asks everyone: *what will you do with the life you get back?* Only an answer he means will do.
- **Powers in play:** none of his. This chapter is about being judged by powers greater than his own. In the Hush nobody speaks.
- **Grows stronger:** he remembers everything: his mother, the lamp test on the Sluice, Rook and the yearly dinners, his climb, the dumping orders with his signature, Site Nine, the engine, the faces of the Makers, the call to Rook, Crane's cable tie and *it's the chairman's order; I asked him twice.*
- **The low point:** the burns on the arms at Low Anthem were his work, and the man who ate soup on Jef's step is the man who raised him and had him drowned. He now also knows Halcyon's codes, routes and plans, and how long until the engine is ready.
- **Maelis's two stories:** Rook at fourteen, the one child the rays carried out of a lost ferry, silent for a year at the Hush, who wanted Jef to build for the islands; and what Jef did at Sounding, which is why he would not. *Jef never spoke of him as a failure. He spoke of him as a thing he had made badly.*
- **Ends with:** the rays agreeing to carry word for him, and Maelis saying: "Jef built worse than you did, once. Go and fix it."
- **Scenes and texture:** four days south on the Dragon with Sefa at the wall and Okonta's drills morning and evening: the dive under the Girdle through the endless grey snow of agravar rubbish, a fleet's keels passing overhead, then the blaze of the Bright Shelves and Nine Spires far off. At Ember House, a cairn with Jef's apron on it. Back on the black sand he tells Sefa and Okonta everything at once; Sefa swims off for three hours and returns to say she is coming to see him pay.

### Chapter 8. Saltgate

- **Where:** Saltgate. Stilts and sea-stacks, canals and locks, neon in the rain, the Wet Market with its stalls half under water.
- **New:** agravar life, and the land itself as a danger: mariner companions dry out within a day and must keep to the canals. On land every drop is orphan water. The Driftfolk and the Clean Hands.
- **Challenge:** Ilan knows Brightwater holds a captured Wellwalker in a clinic tower here. Ilan walks into the tower on his old authority and his codes and brings her out when the alarm goes, with nothing to call on but the water in the sprinkler pipes. Dace and Sefa wait at the loading dock. He walks streets where his own face is on Halcyon notices, listed as lost at sea, and passes the tenement where his mother dried out and died.
- **Powers in play:** the gift at a reach weaker and fading fast, drawn from pipes and tanks. Sefa's intended water, which lasts only hours this far from the sea.
- **Grows stronger:** the Wellwalker teaches him intending. He learns it is the same act as calling, asked with care instead of force, and that it fails whenever he wants something for himself: Mend, Purge, Ease.
- **Brightwater falls:** she and Sefa open a free clinic at the Wet Market and heal anyone who comes. Other healers follow in other ports. Within a month Brightwater's medicine monopoly is gone, and in Clearwell the workers' towns stop buying.
- **Dr Marsh:** on the sixth night Brightwater's chief comes to the table alone. She admits the tank, says she was a doctor before she was a board, and offers to open forty hospitals to the healers at no charge. She cannot do it without the chairman's leave, because the Exchange holds her company's notes. She has asked to see him.
- **Ends with:** a dockworker whose child Sefa healed asking Ilan who he is. He gives his real name for the first time.
- **Scenes and texture:** Dace's patched boat and the crossing by the Eyes, with gale skates overhead and belt sharks astern; in the calm of an Eye, Sefa asks what agravars are really like and whether he liked being one; the Lockkeepers in their glass boxes; Sefa's first sight of neon ("where do they put the dark?") and of mariners and agravars cheating each other cheerfully at the Wet Market; Ilan under his mother's window on the Sluice; Pell of the Clean Hands; Yeva teaching him that intending means wanting nothing for yourself; Garran and Pim; Sefa wanting to see the tank empty, with the lid off.

## Session 5: The offer

### Chapter 9. The engineers

- **Where:** Keelhaven on the Kessler Banks. Cranes, dry docks, welding light all night, shift sirens instead of clocks. Then Lashing, the raft-town, somewhere in the Belt Storms.
- **New:** agravars at their best: fifty thousand engineers who have spent careers trying to copy Maker work from wreckers' scraps.
- **Challenge:** Kessler-Vane is building the hull that will carry Halcyon's engine. Ilan cannot fight a shipyard, so he walks in as what he is, a senior engineer, and makes an offer the board cannot: he will teach the craft to anyone who downs tools. He must win over the yard's chief designer, survive the company's attempt to seize him, and get the defectors out through the storms, hopping from Eye to Eye to reach Lashing.
- **Powers in play:** persuasion and proof. He mends Teodora Raske's dead warm-stone on her own desk. Then, in a net loft, he puts Jef's tool into the hands of the engine designer Hanne and talks her through opening her own hidden lamp without touching it himself. Then storm calling on the escape, strong and wild.
- **Grows stronger:** three hundred and eleven engineers follow him, the first people in six centuries to be taught the craft. He is no longer the only pair of hands.
- **Kessler-Vane falls:** not destroyed, but emptied. The hull launches unfinished.
- **Rook's hand:** Raske remembers the man in grey who put a paper on her desk and said, make an engineer of this one. A purple flimsy reaches every hold: *the mariners' new saviour is the man who chose where to dump.* Dace assumes it is Halcyon's work. Deep Chorus calls a Moot of all the waters, now to try him.
- **Ends with:** a grey launch in Lashing's Eye, one old gentleman with a hamper, who has asked for the law and says his surveyor will know the day.
- **Scenes and texture:** Keelhaven from the sea, sparks falling under an orange sky; the empty design loft at fourth siren; the defectors at the ferry stage carrying their secret Maker scraps wrapped in cloth; Lashing rising and falling on the swell; the first lesson on a lantern-lit barge deck, with forty lamps alight by the tenth day and mariner traders watching in disbelief; Sefa teaching two apprentices to hold their breath.

### Chapter 10. The table

- **Where:** Lashing. The afterdeck of a gutted Meridian frigate, a table, two chairs, a lantern, and an iron grating with the sea under it.
- **New:** the villain at length, and the law of Lashing kept for an enemy. What the engine is, and what ordering is.
- **Challenge:** this chapter is a conversation. Rook has come for their yearly dinner, the twenty-third. He is glad to see Ilan and means it. He admits the order to Crane and gives his reason: Ilan was about to start a war for the city. He admits the dart: he wanted to see if Jef could let a machine go. Then he shows his chart of the Girdle shelf and makes the offer. If Ilan seats the engine properly in its own city, Rook will stop Halcyon's fleet with a letter this week, and nobody need boil anyone. Without him there is only the gun.
- **Powers in play:** none of the hero's. Rook has him dip a soup bowl from the sea, sets the engine's governor in it, and drives it through the Thread. The water stands back in a ring and then lies flat, deaf for good. He explains: *you gave that barge an order and begged its pardon in the same breath. The engine isn't sorry. If you ever need to win, and not merely to mend, remember the bowl.*
- **The temptation:** it is the cleverest and kindest thing Rook has ever offered him, and he is right about the hearts. Ilan wants to say yes for about as long as it takes to pour a glass. He refuses because Rook's chart has nobody on it: *I drew eleven of those.* He has no answer to *and the forty million?*
- **Before leaving, Rook says** that the purple paper was his; that when the Moot has finished with Ilan any Meridian door will take him; and that Linnea Marsh died in a fire at her Clearwell clinic on Tuesday, after asking his leave. *I did not light anything. I never do.*
- **Grows stronger:** he now knows how ordering is done. That is the hook.
- **Ends with:** Sefa, who listened from under the grating, feeling the bowl die like a patient; Okonta asking *what heart?*; and the answer nobody wants to finish: there is only one that large, it runs where no islander can reach it, and it now runs.
- **Scenes and texture:** Odo explaining the law twice; Raske refusing to sit across from him; the bream cooked in stormgrass ash by Dace's aunt, who drops his scrip through the grating with tongs; the forty white lamps on a barge rail two decks away; Sefa's verdict that Rook never once asked Ilan what he wanted, and that Jef asked him things.

## Session 6: The fall and the trial

### Chapter 11. Deaf water

- **Where:** a valley in the kelp of the Anthem Waters, between Fallow and Chorus Canyon.
- **New:** a Meridian "protective patrol" at work. What ordering does.
- **Challenge:** Fallow's lamps are out and its speaker would not mark the paper. Nine hundred people are swimming for Deep Chorus with sleds. Twelve grey ships follow, piloted by Corin, who has been told to bring them in gently. The patrol lays a curtain of refinery spoil across the valley so that nobody can call, then takes the column in trawl nets. Ilan arrives with Okonta and Sefa.
- **Powers in play:** his held skin of water saves him from three charges. In fouled water his asking tears one net and frees eleven people. He tries to Purge the valley and cannot, because he wants the ships dead and the water can feel nothing else in him. A transport is leaving with three hundred people in its tanks.
- **The failure:** he remembers the bowl. He puts everything out of himself except one will and orders the clean sea beyond the curtain: *off them.* Twelve hulls go under in the time it takes to shut a door. The nets part and the transports open like pods.
- **The cost:** the kelp stops humming for ten kilometres around, across the road back to Fallow. A hundred and sixty-one sailors drown; Fallow's people pull a hundred and thirty out alive. The undertow comes for the emptied hero, Sefa holds him as Yeva taught her, and it takes her gift instead.
- **Rook's hand:** Corin was told that if the surveyor came, he was to keep clear and *let him do what he does*. A fast boat with a lens on its mast watched all morning.
- **Hesper:** she watched from the Tangle. *So that's how it's done.* She leaves for the south with more lights than she came with. Told that it took Sefa's gift, she says only: *then it took the wrong one.*
- **Grows stronger:** he does not. He looks at every one of the dead, promises Sefa never to do it again, and does not sleep.
- **Ends with:** the people of Fallow walking on toward the canyon with a hundred and thirty islanders among them in borrowed helmets, not singing.
- **Scenes and texture:** the chord of the forest heard again after months, and then its absence; the old man of Fallow walking easily beside his daughter's sled; Sefa putting her hands into the sea twice and saying *it's just water*, then retying her band with her teeth; Okonta counting the dead, who has never sunk a hull with its crew aboard; the old caller of Fallow who cannot hold both things he will remember.

### Chapter 12. The Great Moot

- **Where:** Tidehall on the Bright Shelves. A drowned amphitheatre a kilometre across, in clear sunlit water over white sand, glass coral chiming in the swell. Nine Spires hosts the speakers.
- **New:** mariner politics at full scale, and how different the holds are from each other: worldly Nine Spires, stubborn Pearl Stair, proud Deep Chorus, blunt Rime. It is the first Moot of all the waters ever held.
- **Challenge:** two charges. The paper: he chose where Halcyon dumps. And Fallow: he gave the sea an order, and ten kilometres of the Anthem Waters are deaf. He admits both without excuse. Tenders want him exiled. Hesper wants him as the Breakers' weapon, or dead. His only argument is what he has done since.
- **Who speaks:** Odile of Nine Spires presides. A dark hold and Pearl Stair call for exile. Elder Vey and Brask of Rime speak for him. Ione speaks without forgiving him: he mends her dome and every dome on the eleven routes first, and her daughter's hands are empty because of him. Sefa speaks last: *he sat up all night with their dead. Hesper watched the same thing and went away pleased. I can't heal any more. I can still tell.*
- **Powers in play:** the Moot's test is a shared calling, sung of and never achieved. Hesper warns him it has pulled every caller who tried it into the undertow.
- **Grows stronger:** he holds it. At Deep Chorus he took and held. At Fallow he emptied himself of everything but his own will. This is the other emptiness: he takes nothing and makes himself a way through. A knee-high swell rolls out from Tidehall and every reef of glass coral for a hundred kilometres rings at once. Nobody is lost.
- **The judgement:** he is bound to the mending of the holds for life, beginning with the eleven routes. He is never again to command the sea; any caller who sees him do it may put him down. The engineers are guests of all the waters. The holds will act together.
- **The split:** Hesper and eleven other Mighty leave in the night for Port Halcyon. Corin's needle is with them. He has told them how a thing can be built quickly.
- **Ends with:** the mariners united in everything except the twelve who matter most.
- **Scenes and texture:** the tug's shadow running over white sand; Nine Spires trading across the tide line in daylight; Raske on the apron at night with a lamp and a ring of curious mariners; the walk out to Tidehall like a fair; afterwards, Ione and Ilan alone on the rim of the bowl.

## Session 7: The signal and the wave

### Chapter 13. The signal

- **Where:** Concord, in the Sovereigns. Glass towers, screens the size of buildings, and the Orrin Spire, the tallest thing on Ovara. They stage from Old Tolla, a fishing village in the Stubborns whose shrine shows that these islanders never forgot their neighbours.
- **New:** how agravars are kept ignorant. Marit Coyle, a reporter who has fed the Clean Hands for years. The Meridian Exchange, windowless, underneath it all.
- **Challenge:** agravars still think mariners are half myth and that "sea terrorists" are sinking their ships. Ilan must get on air long enough to be believed.
- **The trap:** Caspar Venn, who owns the Spire, sends word that he wants out and will give Ilan the main channel himself. Marit trusts none of it, accepts, and has her three people bring up the backup transmitter anyway. In the studio Venn sits beside him, grave and handsome. Six minutes in, Marit shows Ilan what is really going out, seven seconds behind: his own words cut into a claim to lead the "sea terrorists", over the long-lens film of twelve ships going under at Fallow. Venn, hand over his microphone: *he does hold all my notes, you know.*
- **Orrin falls:** Bertil brings the backup up. Every screen on Ovara shows Venn half out of his chair, reaching for the censor's booth. When Meridian soldiers break in, Marit turns a live camera on their captain, and the studio crew steps between the guns and the chair.
- **Powers in play:** almost none. High in a glass tower there is only orphan water. This chapter is won by people.
- **Grows stronger:** he gains a voice. He confesses the routes, Low Anthem and the barge. Then he confesses Fallow, since the film was real: *I'm a man who has now killed people both of the ways there are. With a pen, and with a word. I slept very well after the first. I haven't slept since the second.* He shows the engine and names Anselm Rook, whose face the islands have never been shown.
- **Ends with:** two messages arriving together. Halcyon's fleet has sailed with the engine, two days early. And off Port Halcyon the sea has started to rise, after six days instead of twelve.
- **Scenes and texture:** the shrine at Old Tolla and what it does to Sefa; Marit telling how she found his name; Marit and Sefa on the quay in the rain; the washed streets of Concord with crowds cheering the fleet on the screens; Venn holding Ilan's hand in both of his in the lobby; the long lift ride as the sea falls away from him; Marit's short speech in the make-up room and his answer, *I slept very well*; Bertil unbuttoning his collar as he steps in front of the guns; the captain whose daughter is at the refinery school on the Waterline.

### Chapter 14. The wave

- **Where:** Port Halcyon in the Crown. A city of two million stacked up the inside of a black caldera, ash falling, refineries at the waterline. The harbour is deaf water.
- **New:** the one thing the Dragon has never done.
- **Challenge, part one:** two disasters three thousand kilometres apart. The Dragon runs only pole to pole and has only ever crossed the Girdle deep in its trench. With the Thread, Ilan calls it up and steers it off its course for the first time in three thousand years, running on the surface, with Okonta and fifteen returning Threaded flying escort. He knows what he is bringing into the light, and can think of no other way to reach two million people.
- **Corin:** he tries to bar the way. Okonta meets him prow to prow and shows him the tin-roofed tank-sheds on the refinery quay, where Rook has put Kelder and three other holds: eleven thousand mariners, under the wave. He goes in toward them and she lets him.
- **Part two, the wave:** two hundred metres high, built in six days by Hesper's chorus of twelve. They did not ask for it; each ordered a piece to stand, and now it will not hear them, and nobody can let go. He does not fight them. He tells Hesper whose signature burned her people, offers himself, and takes the twelve wills one at a time into a shared calling. The wave lies down.
- **Part three, the fleet:** Halcyon turns back and fires the lance at him across its own harbour, where the sea will not answer. So he does first what the Wellwalker taught him: he Purges the harbour, healing the water itself until it can hear. Then he raises it as a shield, reaches into the lance with the craft from eleven kilometres and shuts it off. Then he lifts forty warships, asking, and sets them in their own berths with every sailor alive.
- **Powers in play:** everything: the Thread, shared calling, Purge at the scale of a harbour, Shape, Hold, Raise, and the craft at a distance. He ends the chapter ebbed to the bone.
- **Halcyon falls:** its fleet beached, its crimes broadcast, its lance dead. Adler Crane stands on the bridge like a man looking at a bill come due.
- **Ends with:** silence, every camera on him, Sefa's arms round his chest, and through the Thread, which being ebbed does not touch, a needle of an old pattern settling into one of the Dragon's cradles.
- **Scenes and texture:** the silent lane the crowd makes for him in Concord; the run east along the Girdle, the only time he sees the islands whole, with Saltgate's neon dark and its roofs crowded, and Lashing's rails lined with lit Maker lamps; Port Halcyon seen from the water for the first time, Waterline, Stacks and Rim; Hesper folded in the water afterwards; the lieutenant at a destroyer's rail.

## Session 8: Nothing left

### Chapter 15. The heart

- **Where:** the harbour mouth of Port Halcyon, then the refinery school on the Waterline.
- **New:** the hero with nothing: no gift, no Dragon, and an enemy who has won.
- **The failure:** Rook is in the gallery, in his own cradle. Two Threaded pull on the Dragon. Ilan rebuilt its heart; Rook has known it for fifty years as a blind man knows a house. Ilan is ebbed and holds the door with his fingertips. While they contend, Rook's voice speaks from every wall on every island: all debts are called, service will be taken at any Meridian door, and every household that serves will be paid in a deed of new ground, available within the season. Okonta's fifteen are met by more than twenty grey needles; she reaches the Dragon's brow and it will not open to her. The Dragon turns to the open sea. *I brought it to his door.*
- **What he wakes to, three days later:** the Dragon lying in a ring on the sea floor at Site Nine with its heart taken out. A grey wall of ordered water standing round a pit of bare mud that widens in jumps. Saltgate's Wet Market emptied of mariners, and its Lockkeepers measuring the distance to the wall; deaf sea sixty kilometres out and growing; the southernmost holds of the forest falling silent. And the islands queueing gladly at the grey doors.
- **Garran's question:** he has been read a deed, forty paces by twenty, the first ground his family would ever own. *You've told me what's wrong with his. You've not told me where there's another.*
- **Crane's report:** he gives himself up between two fishwives. The engine is two parts: the lance, which Halcyon had, and a ring forty metres across that never left the vault. Rook let the fleet sail to see what Ilan would do to the lance; his watchers could not tell. So he is running the ring as it is, seated wrong. If it stops or fails, a wall sixty metres high comes down in one piece and goes over every island on the Girdle. It cannot be broken. *He never does pretend. I used to think that was a virtue.* Ilan puts him in the third row.
- **Powers in play:** none. Yeva gives him a month before he can call anything larger than a bathtub.
- **Grows stronger:** only in knowing the answer. The city was built to come up: wet halls and dry halls, for people who did both. The ring is not a wall. It is seated wrong, and has been for three thousand years. Raising it would take every caller, every healer and every engineer there is, and boats from the islands to carry them, and he cannot order one of them to come.
- **Ends with:** Sefa: *You'll have to ask. — I'm bad at it. — You're terrible at it. You'll get practice.*
- **Scenes and texture:** the socket where the Dragon's hum used to be; the schoolroom with children's drawings of ships, and one of a house with a figure on a green line and one on a blue; people along the quays looking down into their clean harbour; the captain from Studio One on guard at the door; a woman who kisses his blanket and another who spits; Pim and Bother regarding each other.

### Chapter 16. The long way round

- **Where:** the tank-sheds at Port Halcyon; Saltgate with its canals empty of mariners; the high hall of Deep Chorus; Lashing, drifted to the edge of the dead sea.
- **New:** the hero asking instead of doing. Each stop is a small scene of persuasion with a different kind of people, and each can refuse.
- **The tanks:** eleven thousand mariners in concrete troughs under lamps that do not fail, with brass tags on their wrists. Corin has spent three days sawing off the sluice gates with dockers' saws. Ilan cannot unmark their paper or promise them a home. Kelder holds a Moot in a trough and comes out into a harbour that can hear. Corin says the rule again, to Okonta: *to you. You're alive.*
- **Saltgate:** the mariners have gone and the lower tiers of the Wet Market stand empty. The senior Lockkeeper will not take a side; he asks her only to read out a measurement, the distance from the gates to the wall. *No sea, no gates. That isn't a side. That's arithmetic.* She sounds the horns that are kept for the day the gates shut and sends every gate barge.
- **Deep Chorus:** in the hall that carries every word, he tells twelve thousand people that two of their hearts cannot be mended, that dead water will reach them within the year, that he can do nothing himself, and that he has nothing to offer. Vey: *he never asked us for anything. I have wondered whether that was why we let him carry it.* The forty choirs answer tier by tier. Brask is at the sea-door with forty of Rime and a sledge of cod.
- **The gathering:** the Eyes of the Belt are sliding toward Site Nine. Lashing lies at the edge of the deaf water with boats to the horizon and two hundred holds in the sea around it: forty thousand people looking at each other across a metre of air and water. Forty kilometres west stands the wall.
- **Sefa:** Maelis arrives on the notched ray with a bottle she has sat with for nine days and asks her question. Sefa: *I thought it was mine. If I had it back, I'd know I was only where it went through.* The rays return her gift.
- **What the rays want:** they have carried everyone's memory alone since before the Makers. They want the walls of the city lit, so that they can put something down. Maelis is coming in: *I should like him to see my face.*
- **Hesper:** she has been back to look at the water where her wave stood and at the valley by Fallow. *You can't. We can. You've only to be the place it goes through.* Her twelve are in the first ring.
- **Powers in play:** the Thread only. He can ride. Everything else is done by other people: Odile's lists, Vey's choirmasters, Odo's trumpet.
- **Grows stronger:** he watches himself stop being indispensable, and finds it is like setting down a sled rope.
- **Ends with:** Garran looking at the wall. *Is that it? The other field? — It's under that. — Then we'd best go and fetch it.*
- **Scenes and texture:** a docker's wife handing bread rolls down to mariner children who have never seen bread; the noodle-seller on the Sluice who knew his parents; the Lockkeeper's logbook the size of a paving stone; the people of Kelder keeping their brass tags on until afterwards; Crane in a borrowed helmet holding a lamp for Hanne.

## Session 9: The turning

### Chapter 17. Site Nine

- **Where:** forty kilometres of dead sea, a wall of ordered water sixty metres high, a pit of steaming mud eighty kilometres across, and the drowned city at its centre with the Dragon lying dead in a ring round it.
- **New:** restoring deaf water, which nobody has done. The Makers' own city, and the oldest secret in the world.
- **Part one, the road:** six thousand healers sit down at the edge of the dead water. Purging is no use; there is nothing in it to take out. The healers of the Hush show them how to Restore: sit with it, hands in, and want nothing. After an hour a hand's width hears. It quickens as the living sea behind them leans in. The islanders give it speed: dockers lift the tired healers aboard, sit them along the gunwales with their arms over the side, and row. In two days and a night a road of living water a kilometre wide reaches the wall.
- **Opposition:** Rook's twenty-two Threaded come down on the line. Corin goes out with his hands open and calls them by name: *where did he put your mother?* Thirteen come over. A Meridian gunboat blocks the barges; Garran speaks to its crew through Odo's trumpet about what they have both been promised, with Marit's camera on them and the Lockkeepers' horns sounding. The crest comes down.
- **Part two, the wall:** it cannot be called or crossed. Thirty needles fly over it, two to a needle, carrying engineers. Ilan rides alone. He steps onto the mud of the city barefoot, ebbed, with one tool, and the lamps light ahead of him down the street. Crane stands the vault guards down.
- **Part three, the vault:** Rook sits at a bench among hundreds of drawings with his hands in bloodied rags. He has opened one seam, on a Tuesday, after forty-nine years; it was the wrong one. Maelis: *you never could wait for the other person's stone.* Ilan says he has come to seat the engine, not for Rook but right, and tells him what it is for and that there were hundreds of cities. *It isn't ground. — No. It's better. It's somewhere to go.* Rook picks up the governor: one more step and the wall is over the barges. Ilan has nothing to stop him with. He holds out Jef's tool, handle first, and offers to teach him.
- **What decides it:** Rook will not take the tool. The archive wall behind him lights and shows the ring used rightly; then a Maker with a square, clever, frightened face forcing it; then the dead sea, the wall falling, the three doors shut, and the people parting on a shore. Rook asks for the figure if it comes down, hears his own answer, and puts the governor on the bench. *Seat it.*
- **Powers in play:** the craft, at the scale of a sea. It takes eleven hours, with Hanne on the lamp, Raske calling sections and Crane fetching. The grey water in the ring turns bright. The wall does not fall: it sinks a hand's width at a time, the sea walks back across the mud at the pace of a tide, and the healers meet it so that it comes in hearing.
- **Rook's end:** the vault fills first. He cannot breathe water and will not put his helmet on. *I don't care to go up and be forgiven by a porter.* Maelis folds her coat and sits down beside him at the rim: *it's what the Hush is for.*
- **Grows stronger:** in the square at dawn, knee deep in the returning sea, he is the way through for everyone. The Moot's callers, every Touched net-mender of two hundred holds, the tagged callers of Kelder, Hesper's twelve. The rays lend the memory of how the seabed lay. Three hundred engineers wake the lamps and air-hearts, and the city helps. It rises until half stands dry and half lies in clear water. Nobody goes under: the healers keep the callers from giving more than they have. He is the mightiest mariner who ever lived, and it is visibly a power made of other people.
- **The truth:** every wall wakes and Marit puts it on every screen: the Makers' faces, both peoples in one, the quarrel in the vault, and the long forgetting. A welder reaches down and a net-mender reaches up. Garran lifts Ione onto a gate barge.
- **Ends with:** Maelis coming up the vault stair alone at noon with her coat over her arm, saying nothing of it, and remarking that the third tower is out of true.
- **Scenes and texture:** Ilan in the stern, shut out of the sea, knowing exactly what six thousand people doing nothing are doing; a docker's wife putting bread into a healer's mouth; Hesper touching the wall, like the face of someone who died angry; Raske riding behind Corin with her eyes shut; the smell of the pit, the wrecks and whale bones, the drilling platform absurd on its six legs in a plain; two old people sitting side by side in the light of a wall, like children on a dock.

### Chapter 18. Meet

- **Where:** the risen city; Low Anthem; the city again in spring.
- **Meridian falls:** its debts are priced on scarce land, and its deeds are for ground that is under the sea again. Within the thirty days three million people walk away from what they owe, into a city no corporation owns. Its chairman is not seen again, and since nobody was ever shown his face, nobody can say what is missing. Under the Exchange there is an empty cradle of an old pattern.
- **The court:** both peoples sit in Tidehall, with Odile keeping the lists. Crane tells it everything. Venn sends a beautifully worded statement. Marsh's successor opens the wards and says it was always her intention.
- **The Dragon:** the ring rests once the city stands. Ilan takes the two wheels out and seats them in the Dragon again, with the Threaded holding lamps.
- **Low Anthem:** before anything else he goes back and mends its dome, with ten Keelhaven engineers and ten mariners of Deep Chorus. It takes forty days. He lights the ten dead lamps with Jef's tool. On the last evening Hesper, who has not sung since Davin died, teaches him the tending song again.
- **Grows stronger:** in a sealed workshop under the eastern quarter the archive walls still read, and show how to Make. He has finished the housing of his first air-heart, and it is true.
- **Ends with:** Maelis arriving on the Dragon to walk the wet streets and tell him the third tower is still out of true. He teaches a class every morning, anyone who passes must teach one, and Adler Crane is in the third row. Sefa wears a Wellwalker's grey. Maelis asks her question again. He rolls up his sleeves.

## What is left unsolved

Deliberately, so the world stays playable afterwards:

- The sea is still poisoned. The Slick and eleven places like it are still spreading.
- The valley south of Fallow is still deaf. Forty healers of the Hush sit with it in turns; they have brought back the width of a terrace and say it will take a hundred years.
- Two of Deep Chorus's hearts, and many like them, want making, and one man has just begun to learn how.
- Some agravars and some mariners will never forgive Ilan Voss. A hundred and sixty-one families have particular cause.
- The corporations are broken, not gone, and their people still need to eat. Twenty-odd Threaded once took Rook's wages.
- There are thirty-seven million islanders still on their rocks, four hundred drowned cities whose places only the rays remember, and one ring that knows how to raise one.
- The rays have put something down. Nobody knows what they will do with the room.
- Nobody knows why the gift and the craft met in one man, or whether it will happen again.
