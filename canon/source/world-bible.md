# Ovara — World Bible

*A water world of two estranged peoples, for use as an RPG setting.*

This document describes the world **as the campaign opens**: Jef is alive, the holds are quietly failing, and nobody has heard of Ilan Voss. What changes during play is in the campaign document, *The Man the Tide Brought In*.

**Contents:** 1 At a glance · 2 Shape, sky and sea · 3 History · 4 Agravars · 5 Mariners · 6 The gift (magic) · 7 The craft (Maker technology) · 8 Jef, the Queen and the Dragon · 9 The great rays · 10 People in between · 11 Gazetteer · 12 Flora and fauna · 13 Conflicts and secrets · 14 Running games here · 15 Decisions made

## 1. The world at a glance

Ovara is an ocean world, smaller and denser than Earth. Nearly all of its surface is water. Land exists only as islands, strung along one stretch of the equator.

- **Agravars** (about 40 million) live on the land. They have roughly year-2000 technology, are ruled by warring mega-corporations, and dump their waste into the sea.
- **Mariners** (about 2 million, in some 300 settlements) live in ancient cities under the ocean. They breathe water, farm the sea, and inherit technology far beyond the agravars' that they can operate but not repair.
- **The great rays** are a third, older people of the deep, who keep to themselves.

The agravars have never dared open war on the mariners, because of the Tidecallers, the healing water, and Jef. That restraint is wearing thin.

**The core tension:** the agravars are poisoning the sea the mariners live in, the mariners' ancient cities are failing, and the one person who can still repair them is very old.

## 2. Shape, sky and sea

Ovara is a globe about 7,600 km across, a good deal smaller than Earth. It is dense and rich in iron, so things weigh about two-thirds of what they would there. Its poles are called the **Tips** and its equator the **Girdle**. The regions run in bands from north to south:

| Band | Region | Character | Places |
|---|---|---|---|
| North Tip | **The Pale** | Cold, still, luminous | Kettle House, the Lantern, Rime |
| North Shoulder | **The Anthem Waters** | Golden kelp forest | Low Anthem, Deep Chorus |
| Equator | **The Girdle** | Hot, stormy, crowded | The corporate islands, Saltgate |
| South Shoulder | **The Bright Shelves** | Shallow, sunlit, sandy | Nine Spires, Tidehall |
| South Tip | **The Quiet** | Dark, warm, volcanic | The Hush, the rays, Ember House |

All the land lies on one shallow shelf along about 3,500 km of the Girdle. The rest of the Girdle is open storm.

- **Days** last about 18 hours. The fast spin drives the **Belt Storms**, permanent bands of wind and rain around the Girdle.
- **The Eyes** are calm windows that drift through the Belt Storms on a known pattern. Surface ships cross the Girdle by hopping from Eye to Eye; missing one can cost a week or the ship.
- **The Tips** see half a year of twilight and half a year of low, sideways sun.
- **The Wells** are two vast trenches, one at each Tip, the deepest water in the world. A great hospital stands at the bottom of each.
- **Travel is slow.** A ship takes months from Girdle to Tip and often does not arrive. Most people never leave the waters they were born in.

## 3. History

Nobody alive knows this in full.

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| Over 3,000 years ago | **The Makers**, one advanced people, covered Ovara. They built the undersea cities, the Dragon and the buildings in the Wells, and altered some of their own to breathe water. |
| About 3,000 years ago | **The Sinking.** The sea rose or the land fell. The altered went below; the rest clung to the islands. |
| After | **The Long Forgetting.** Below, the machines kept working, so knowledge of them faded into ritual. Above, the survivors lost everything and rebuilt from nothing. At some point in these centuries **the gift** appeared among the mariners. |
| About 900 years ago | **The Hold Wars**, the last time mariners fought each other, begun over a kelp boundary. A nineteen-year-old mechanic called Jef, born in the hold of Sounding and raised at the Lantern, had taught himself the craft from its archive walls. He built armed darts for his own hold. Eleven holds were destroyed; Sounding was the last, broken by two of his own darts in enemy hands. He swore never again to build anything that could not be used to keep someone alive. Since that day mariners have had no army, and no hold has commanded another. |
| About 600 years ago | The last hold mechanic other than Jef dies. From here on, only Jef can repair Maker work. |
| About 200 years ago | **The Charter Wars.** Agravar trading houses swallow the island governments and declare the islands corporate property. |
| Now | The corporations have run out of land to take from each other, and are looking down. |

## 4. The agravars

**Life on the islands.** Crowded, vertical, loud. Every square metre of land is owned, so cities grow upward and outward on stilts and floating platforms. There are cars, ships, aircraft that rarely dare the Belt Storms, television, early networks. A citizen belongs to a corporation as people elsewhere belong to a country: it houses them, pays them in its own scrip, and conscripts them.

**Culture.** Fast fashion, neon, synthetic fabrics, hard-edged electronic music, celebrity, advertising on every surface. Agravars prize making, owning and novelty. Most have never seen a mariner and think of them as primitive fish-people or as a fairy tale.

**The waste.** Land is too valuable for landfill, so everything goes into the sea. Household rubbish simply sinks: under the Girdle it falls without end, like grey snow, and lies in drifts on the trench floor. Industrial waste is carried north and south by barge and dropped at night, in deep water "where no one is looking". The barges come most often in storm season.

| Corporation | Business | Home | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Halcyon Dredge & Mineral** | Seabed mining, fuel | Port Halcyon, the Crown | The worst polluter; already drilling near mariner waters. Chief Surveyor Ilan Voss (lost at sea); security chief Adler Crane |
| **Kessler-Vane Shipworks** | Ships, weapons, engines | Keelhaven, the Kessler Banks | Builds everyone's navies; wants Maker vehicle technology above all. Chief designer Teodora Raske |
| **Brightwater Provisions** | Food, drinking water, medicine | Clearwell, the Green Terraces | Smiling public face; secretly pays for stolen healing water. Led by Dr Linnea Marsh, a physician who began in a dockside dispensary |
| **Meridian Assurance** | Banking, insurance, mercenaries | Concord, the Sovereigns | Owns the debts of the others; wars are its product. Its chairman, Anselm Rook, has never been shown in public |
| **Orrin Signal** | Media, networks, surveillance | Concord, the Sovereigns | Decides what agravars believe about the sea. Its chairman, Caspar Venn, has a beautiful voice and no opinion the Exchange has not approved |

Below them are dozens of minor houses, the free ports, and the Stubborns: small islands that never accepted corporate rule.

**The Meridian Scholarship.** Once a year Meridian tests the top form of the dock schools across the Girdle and pays for one child's whole education. Every dock child has heard of it and almost none has won it. Among the objects on the examiner's tray is a short grey rod of Maker work. The examiner watches the children's hands.

**Meridian refuges.** White ships stand over holds whose lamps have failed and let down a glass bell on a chain. The man in it offers passage for the whole hold to a tank-town on an island shore: concrete troughs of piped sea water under electric lamps that do not fail. The price is twenty years' service from every person. Refugees wear a numbered brass tag on a wrist cord. The water in the tanks is orphan water. The largest tank-town stands behind the sea-wall on the Waterline at Port Halcyon.

## 5. The mariners

**The body.** Mariners breathe water as easily as air, a trait the Makers bred into their ancestors. The Makers bred them for the deep as well: a mariner can go from the surface to the floor of a Well and back in a day and take no harm from the weight of the water. Shallowborn usually inherit this. Islanders do not, and cannot go much deeper than the kelp grows without a hard suit, which keeps the pressure of the surface inside it. On land mariners dry out and tire within a day. They tend to be long-limbed, with large dark eyes suited to dim water.

**Holds.** Mariners live in holds: Maker-built domes, towers and caverns, part flooded and part dry, lit and warmed by machines no one can rebuild. The air in a dry hall is not surface air. The air-hearts make a mixture that a mariner can breathe safely at that hold's depth, which is one more reason a failing heart empties a hold. Holds range from a few hundred people to fifty thousand. Each is led by its elders; holds in the same waters send speakers to a regional **Moot**. A Moot does not vote. The speakers talk, for days if need be, until nobody is left who will not go along; if they cannot agree, nothing is done. There is no mariner nation, king or army, and there has never been a Moot of all the waters.

**Daily life.** Work is done in the water. Eating, sleeping, cooking and argument are done in the dry halls, where a fire will burn and a voice does not carry to every ear. A hold is entered through its **sea-door**, a round pool in the floor with sea on one side of its surface and air on the other. Mariners can live in water for days but not for weeks; the old and the very young need the dry halls. The hours are kept by the **day-song**: a singer at the sea-door sings the turn of each watch and the terraces pass it outward. Food is pressed kelp cake, smoked silverback, sea-grapes and cured sea-pear.

**The long-call.** News travels by song, relayed from hold to hold: a few short phrases, sung loud, taken up by the next hold's singer. A long-call can cross the Anthem Waters in three days. It carries a fact and nothing else, which mariners say is why it has lasted.

**The dead** are wrapped in sea-silk, weighted with a stone from the wall of their hold, and laid at the foot of a kelp stem. Their name is cut into the stem at head height and rises as the kelp grows.

**Culture.** Slow, communal, oral. Music is sung and carries for kilometres. Clothing is woven sea-silk, shell and Maker-salvage worn as heirlooms. Mariners prize tending, memory and patience. They treat Maker machines with ritual care, which keeps the machines running and keeps anyone from experimenting with them.

**The burden.** Every hold spends a growing share of its labour netting, sealing and hauling away agravar waste. **Tenders** clean and endure: they net the fall, seal it in bladders of cured hide for the carrying crews, and plant bitterwrack to drink what they missed. **Breakers** want to strike back; they wear a knotted cord at the wrist. The split runs through every hold.

**The Wellwalkers.** Healers of the two great hospitals take turns travelling the world as wandering doctors, treating whoever they meet, mariner or agravar, and asking nothing. They travel alone, carry nothing and never stay. There are never more than a few dozen on the road. Meeting one is the luck of a lifetime. Corporations pay bounties for one alive.

## 6. The gift

The gift is true magic. The Makers did not make it and no machine can copy it.

### How it works

Mariners say *the sea answers*. The power is not in the person; it is in the living sea, and a gifted person asks for it. Three things are needed:

1. **Contact.** The caller must be touching water that is joined to the sea.
2. **Intention.** One clear purpose, held without wavering. A divided mind gets nothing.
3. **Standing.** The sea answers those who live in it and tend it. The first rule taught to every child with the gift is *ask, do not order*.

The gift runs in mariner blood. No agravar has ever had it. Shallowborn children inherit it rarely, and usually weakly.

### The two ways

Calling and intending are the same act done differently: calling asks the sea to *move*, intending asks it to *care*. Almost nobody is strong in both.

| Calling (Tidecallers) | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Listen** | Feel what the water touches: shapes, movement, life, at a distance. Everyone with the gift starts here. |
| **Move** | Push and pull: currents, speed for a swimmer or boat, turning a net aside. |
| **Shape** | Give water form: walls, air pockets, blades, ropes, a sphere held in air. |
| **Hold** | Make water still and unyielding: pin a hull, calm a storm patch, stop a fall. |
| **Raise** | Lift water against its nature: columns, waves, a ship carried on a pillar. |

| Intending (healers) | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Mend** | Close wounds, knit bone, stop bleeding. |
| **Purge** | Draw out poison, infection or filth, from a body or from water itself. |
| **Ease** | Take away pain and fever; give sleep. |
| **Restore** | Regrow what is lost and cure what is long-rooted. Only reliably possible at the Wells. At the Hush it is used on minds: the healer sits with the patient and wants nothing, not even a cure. |

Intended water cannot raise the dead or cure old age. It fades within days once the healer's attention leaves it, so it cannot be bottled and sold.

### How strong

| Reach | Who | What they can do |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Touched** | About one mariner in ten | A room's worth of water: an air bubble, a nudged current, a cut closed |
| 2. **Caller / healer** | About one in a hundred | A boat's worth: stop a skiff, wall off a net, cure a fever |
| 3. **The Mighty** | About one per hold | A ship's worth: stop a vessel, raise a wave over a deck, save the dying |
| 4. **A chorus** | Several Mighty working side by side | A city's worth, but only built slowly over days |
| 5. **Unheard of** | Nobody, yet | A sea's worth |

In a **chorus**, each caller holds one part of a shared work. It adds their strength but is fragile: if one lets go, the whole falls. A true **shared calling**, many callers' strength passing through a single will, is spoken of in songs and has never been achieved. Everyone who has tried to carry other people's strength has gone into the undertow, and some took the others with them. The songs say the one at the centre must take nothing and ask for nothing, only give what passes through a direction.

Children are taught the forms in order, beginning with Listen. Most callers never reach Raise.

**In a fight** there is no time to ask; it is over before a caller has finished saying please. Callers who fight do their asking beforehand. They Hold a skin of water an arm's length about them, already listening, and carry it all day like a shield. When something comes, they have only to lean. Strength is not fighting: three practised Threaded can carry off the strongest caller alive if he is not ready.

### What changes the answer

| Condition | Effect |
|---|---|
| Clean, living sea | Full strength |
| **Fouled water** | One reach weaker |
| **Deaf water** (dead zones, the worst harbours, and any water that has been ordered) | No answer at all |
| **Orphan water** (tanks, pipes, bottles, anything cut off from the sea) | One reach weaker, and fades fast. This is why mariners are weak on land. |
| Storm and rain | Stronger but wild; easy to raise, hard to hold |
| At the Wells | One reach stronger. Healers say it is the oldest water in the world, and nothing has ever been put in it but care. |

### What it costs

- **The ebb.** After real effort the caller is ebbed: cold, shaking, parched, unable to call. It lasts hours for small work and days for great work.
- **Ordering.** The first rule, *ask, do not order*, exists because the sea can be commanded. A caller who puts everything out of themselves except one will, with no doubt, no pity and no please, is obeyed at once and exactly. It takes a breath and costs no effort. The water that obeyed goes deaf and does not answer again. The caller is left empty, which is what the undertow looks for; if someone is holding the caller back, the undertow may take from them instead. Ordering is not taught and has no name among callers. The Makers' great engine works the same way.
- **Two kinds of deaf.** Water deafened by filth can be purged, by a healer strong enough who wants nothing for themselves. Water deafened by an order has nothing in it to take out. It can only be restored: many healers sitting with it, hands in, asking nothing of it, a hand's width at a time. The healers of the Hush know the way of it. It is slow at first and quickens as the living sea behind them leans in to help.
- **The undertow.** A caller who reaches beyond their strength, or lets go of everything in terror, is pulled under. The sea takes something and keeps it: a memory, a sense, a year of life. Mariners say the sea never loses what it is given, and the rays say the same. What the undertow takes can sometimes be given back, but only by the rays.

## 7. The craft

The craft is the Makers' technology. It is not magic and anyone could learn it, if anyone were left to teach it.

| Level | Who |
|---|---|
| **Tend** (clean, operate by ritual) | Every hold |
| **Mend** (diagnose, repair) | Jef alone, for six hundred years |
| **Make** (build new) | Jef alone |

**What the Makers left**

- **Lamps, air-hearts and warm-stones:** the light, air and heat of every hold. An **air-heart** is a pale ribbed shell that opens and closes a few times an hour: it draws the foulness out of the seawater passing through it and breathes clean air into the dry halls. Small ones serve a room; the great ones, the size of a house, serve a whole hold. There are about two thousand in the world, all the same age. A **warm-stone** is a dark ovoid the size of a loaf that heats a room; it is the simplest thing the Makers built.
- **Archive walls:** surfaces of light that hold records and show, in moving pictures, how each Maker thing was built. Almost all are dark or unreadable. The last in the Lantern went out about six hundred years ago, and Jef never learned to mend them.
- **Calling stones:** about forty six-sided pillars on the seabed, strung along the lines the Dragon runs. A stone sings one low note when the Dragon is within a day.
- **Vehicles:** needles (a sliver of pale metal three times a man's length, ridden lying along it, very fast, with room for one passenger), darts (armed), deep-runners (stout and slow, built for the trenches and used for the Wells).
- **The Dragon** (section 8).
- **Deep works:** things the Makers sealed away, including at least one engine that can boil a sea.

**The Thread.** An implant, made only by Jef: a filament finer than a hair, laid along the nerves from the temple to the base of the skull in a night's work. It leaves thin pale scars down the sides of the neck and aches for a week. It lets a person steer Maker vehicles as an extension of their body, hear through the hull for some eighty kilometres, and feel every working Maker thing nearby as a faint constant hum. Those who carry it are **the Threaded**. Any willing body can take the Thread, agravar included.

**How Maker things work.** They are not built like agravar machines. Agravar machines push; Maker things flow, from somewhere to somewhere. When one stops it is silted up, seated wrong or worn through, and mending is clearing, seating or replacing. Each opens along a seam that only a trained hand can find. Jef works with a set of small hand tools taken from a store at the Lantern.

**Why it matters.** The gift and the craft have never lived in one person. Tidecallers cannot make sense of machines, and Jef has no gift. Nobody knows whether this is a law or only a habit.

## 8. Jef, the Queen and the Dragon

### Jef

To most mariners Jef is a living god. In truth he is a mariner about nine hundred years old, the last person who understands the craft. He lives simply, dislikes being worshipped, and has no gift. He has kept himself alive with a Maker medicine machine he found in the archive walls, which he has mended as often as it will stand.

He was born in Sounding and carried to the Lantern at six with a wasting fever. Nobody came to fetch him, because his hold was at war, so he stayed, swept the halls, and spent eleven years in front of the archive walls while they could still be read. What he did at nineteen is in section 3. He does not speak of it. He has had eight wives, has trained sixty apprentices, none of whom could carry the craft away (the fifty-ninth carried off everything else; see section 13), and has begged the holds for centuries to send him their cleverest children instead of their most devout.

He does not rule. He repairs what he can reach, decides who may ride the Dragon, and makes the Threaded.

### The Threaded

Jef's warrior monks: about a hundred individuals today, where there were two hundred a generation ago, alone or in twos and threes, sworn to a simple rule of life and to keeping agravar intrusion out of mariner waters. The rule is three lines, said once to Jef and never mentioned again: *Keep the water. Own nothing you cannot carry. Come when you are called.* A candidate first serves a year at the Lantern. The Threaded turn back survey boats, cut trawl lines, pull people from wrecks on both sides, and carry the sick to the Dragon for nothing. Jef expects them to sink no hull with its crew aboard. He has laid no Thread for six years. They are not an army and answer only to Jef. Most are mariners; a very few are agravars, which scandalises both sides. Mariners tell of one Threaded who went up to the islands before anyone now living was born and never came down. The Thread is not the gift: being ebbed does not touch it.

### The Dragon

A Maker transit machine that most take for a living creature: a kilometre of pale metal ribs wound through with bound, racing water, circling the world from Tip to Tip without ever stopping. It has kept the same pattern for three thousand years. A pole-to-pole run takes nearly five days.

Inside, it is one flooded hall a kilometre long, the **gallery**: warm, still water under arching ribs, with the racing bound water for walls, so that riders look out at the sea as through the back of a waterfall. Travellers have hung hammock-nets and scratched their names there for three thousand years, and a row of two hundred cradles in the belly holds the Threaded's vehicles, each with its rider's name cut beside it. One near the end has stood empty for decades, beside a short square island name that somebody once tried to scratch out. Most riders are the sick, bound for the Wells. Its heart, amidships under the gallery floor, is three nested wheels, each as wide across as a table, that turn plain sea into bound water. It is the largest heart on Ovara.

It never stops, but at each Tip it rounds the Well at walking pace, and riders step off onto the landing as it passes. It crosses the Girdle a kilometre down, in the trenches beside the island shelf, and has never surfaced or turned aside there; there is no calling stone between the two Shoulders. Pale singers often run alongside it in the north.

It was built to serve cities all round the world, so on each circuit it steps its line a little to one side and passes over a different stretch of sea. Mariners keep **dragon almanacs** and gather at calling stones when it is due. Riders match its speed in a Threaded vehicle and show a token from Jef; it opens or it does not. Jef tells it whom to let in. Any Threaded can feel it from far off; one who knows it well enough could ask it to turn, though in three thousand years nobody has. The almanacs have been wrong more often lately. The Dragon is slowing.

### Maelis, Queen of the Stingrays

Jef's wife of fifty-one years, now in her seventies. She was born in the Quiet and trained as a healer at the Hush. For a year, when she was seventeen, she shared the Hush with a shipwrecked island boy the rays had brought in, three years younger than herself, who taught her a game played with stones. At twenty she was sent to gather the spines the rays shed, and one of them looked at her for a day. The great rays have chosen her as the one person they speak through; "queen" is the mariners' word for that, and she calls herself their mouth. She makes potions said to cure anything, from ray venom, growths of the southern vents and intended water. Unlike ordinary healing water, her potions keep. She gives them rarely and always asks first: *what will you do with the life you get back?*

### Where they live

Together, at both ends of the world, on no schedule. **Kettle House** stands on the lip of the north Well; **Ember House** on a black-sand islet above the south Well. At either house anyone may wait on the step, and nobody goes into the workshop. They ride the Dragon between them as they please. A pilgrim may reach one and find it empty. The rays always know where they are, and sometimes tell. They never travel into the Girdle, and neither grasps how bad things have become there.

## 9. The great rays

A third people: huge, long-lived, older than the Makers. A grown ray is the size of a ship. They do not use words or tools and build nothing.

- **Their gift is memory.** The rays hold what the sea remembers. They can put a memory into a person as one puts a cup into a hand, showing the past as the water knew it, and they are the keepers of whatever the undertow takes. They can send a picture to someone far away when they choose.
- **Their home** is the Wheel, in the Quiet, though they range the whole deep.
- **Their aims** are their own. They have watched the Makers rise and sink. They have not said what they think of the present.
- **Their venom** kills in seconds and is the base of Maelis's potions. Healers take it from the tail spines the rays shed.
- **They have never entered the Girdle.**

## 10. People in between

- **Driftfolk.** Agravars with no corporation, living on raft-towns that follow the Eyes through the Belt Storms. They know the pattern of the Eyes by heart, read the weather from the gale skates, carry what the corporations will not, and print their notices in cheap purple ink. They trade with both sides and are trusted by neither.
- **Shallowborn.** Children of mariner and agravar parents. Some breathe water, some cannot, some only for a while. They tolerate land far better than mariners do. Many hide what they are.
- **Wreckers.** Salvagers who dive on drowned Maker ruins for technology to sell to Kessler-Vane.
- **The Clean Hands.** An agravar underground that sabotages dumping and smuggles information to the holds: clerks, stokers and a few doctors who see the tonnage sheets and cannot sleep. It has no leader and no meetings, which is why its members are alive.

## 11. Gazetteer

Each region has its own light, sound and danger. Places are listed north to south.

### The Pale (north Tip)

*Cold, mirror-still water under a twilight sky. Everything here glows. The danger is the cold and the depth.*

- **Kettle House.** Jef and Maelis's north home: a low stone house on the lip of the Well, half in the water and half out, with a seaweed garden edged in shells, a washing line, a kettle on the step and a workshop full of half-mended things. No guards. The Dragon's landing is a short walk down.
- **The Lantern.** The northern hospital, at the bottom of the Well: nine great halls of milk-pale Maker glass set in a ring and joined by covered ways, lit from inside, visible from far above as a single star in black water. One great heart under the centre of the ring feeds all nine. Four halls have been dark for centuries. Patients float in pools of intended water, each tended by three healers in shifts so the intention never lapses. About nine hundred healers work here.
- **The North Well.** A descent of nine hours through water too cold and heavy for most. Its upper waters are lit by lantern jellies and glowing weed; healers bring patients down on litters slung between two jellies. Giant squid called well-wardens circle it and let through only those travelling with a healer or a token.
- **Rime.** The only hold of the Pale: a dome hung upside down from the underside of the ice shelf, glowing amber, home to six hundred hunters and ice-divers. They feed the Lantern for nothing and the Lantern mends them for nothing, and nobody has written it down. They also see that nobody starves on Jef's doorstep. Blunt, generous people in sealskin who think the Shoulder holds are soft.

### The Anthem Waters (north Shoulder)

*Green-gold light falling through kelp sixty metres tall. The forest hums in the current, and the mariners here sing with it. The danger is getting lost, and what dumping has done to the forest's edge.*

- **Low Anthem.** A small farming hold of four hundred: one dome with half its lamps dark and lampweed strung on the dark side, stone-walled kelp terraces all round, a meeting hall at the centre with a dead Maker lamp that people touch for luck. Poor, close-knit, mostly Tenders. It sits under one of Halcyon's dumping routes.
- **Fallow.** The next hold to Low Anthem, a day away, and the first link in its long-call. Nine hundred people, an old Mighty, and lamps that are nearly all gone. Five days' swim from Deep Chorus with children.
- **Kelder.** A hold of eleven hundred north of the canyon, already dark. Its speaker has marked Meridian's paper.
- **Deep Chorus.** The oldest hold in the north, built down both walls of a canyon a kilometre wide: hundreds of glass-fronted galleries, tier under tier, joined by slender bridges and hung with weed gardens. Every voice echoes, so that forty singers sound like four hundred. Twelve thousand people, forty choirs, proud of their age. Anything said in the elders' high hall carries to every gallery. Seven great air-hearts lie in vaults at the canyon's foot; they are failing and the elders are hiding it, and the lowest tiers, which send no speakers, suffer first.
- **Greywatch Stone.** A calling stone on a bare rise above the forest: a ring of worn benches, a pillar, and centuries of names scratched by people who waited.
- **The Tangle.** Wild, unfarmed kelp, dark and thick, littered with agravar wrecks from the Charter Wars. Breakers hide here. So do things that should not be this far north.
- **Sounding.** A ruin in the Tangle: a dome three times the size of Low Anthem's, broken open like an egg nine hundred years ago. Six thousand people lived there. Nothing grows on it, and the kelp leaves a ring of bare sand around it. Jef's birthplace.
- **The Slick.** A dead zone in the kelp south-east of Low Anthem, where a current has settled twenty years of dumping; the carrying crews bring their sealed bladders here too, having nowhere else. Its border is a wall of dead bitterwrack a hundred metres deep. Black bitterwrack, no fish, no sound. It is deaf water: the gift does not answer here at all.

### The Girdle (equator)

*Heat, rain, wind and crowds. The sea is warm and murky and the sky is rarely clear. The danger is the storms and the people.*

- **Port Halcyon** (the Crown: black volcanic islands, rich in ore). A city of two million stacked up the inside of a flooded caldera in three bands: the **Waterline** (refineries, quays, tank farms and workers' barracks under a brown haze), the **Stacks** (a thousand grey tenement blocks whose streets are stairs) and the **Rim** (gardens, white houses and clean air). Ash falls most days; on the Rim, men are paid to sweep it. The harbour is deaf water. Behind the sea-wall on the refinery quay stand two hundred Meridian tank-sheds with tin roofs (see section 4).
- **Keelhaven** (the Kessler Banks: low sand islands built over edge to edge). A shipyard that is also a city of three hundred thousand. Hundreds of slipways, cranes, an orange sky of welding light all night, and six shift sirens a day instead of clocks. Its fifty thousand engineers are the best-educated agravars alive and privately obsessed with Maker salvage.
- **Clearwell** (the Green Terraces: high, wet, fertile islands). Brightwater's showpiece: white terraces, hanging farms, fountains, clean streets. Everything is pleasant and everything is watched. The workers' towns on the leeward side are not on the postcards.
- **Concord** (the Sovereigns: three small islands joined by bridges). The neutral capital where the five boards meet. Glass towers, washed streets, guarded trees, screens the size of buildings, private security at every door. The **Orrin Spire**, two hundred and twelve floors of blue glass, is the tallest thing on Ovara; the **Meridian Exchange** at its foot fills a block and has one door and no windows. Under it is a flooded dock that only the chairman uses.
- **Saltgate.** The great free port, built on a hundred sea-stacks and the stilts and cables strung between them, in the gap between corporate waters. Its only law is the **Lockkeepers**, who own the great iron lock-gates, take the same toll from everyone and a side for no one. Canals and locks let mariners swim into the heart of the city, to the **Wet Market**: a roofed basin where six canals meet, with three tiers of stalls, one dry, one awash at high tide and one a metre under water. **The Sluice** is a poor back canal of leaning tenements. Neon, smoke, every language, every spy.
- **Lashing.** The largest Driftfolk raft-town: six thousand hulls, barges and platforms roped together on a mat of stormgrass a kilometre wide, with streets of planking, rope bridges, gardens in tubs, a market in a gutted frigate and a soup barge at its centre. It lives in an Eye and moves with it. Its one law is that nobody who reaches it is ever handed back. The law is asked for in set words, *I've reached you, and I ask not to be handed back*, and it binds both ways: nobody may lay a hand on the one who asked, and he may lay a hand on nobody. It has been given to hunter and hunted on the same day. It is never in the same place twice and has never been taken.
- **The Stubborns.** Forty or fifty small, steep islands off the western end of the Girdle's shelf that never signed a charter. Fishing villages, terraced plots, old shrines to the sea. Poor, free and suspicious of everyone.
- **Old Tolla.** One of the Stubborns: forty turf-roofed stone houses on a crag above a six-boat cove. Its shrine holds a worn carving of a long-limbed, large-eyed woman rising from a wave, whom the villagers call "the neighbours" and leave the first fish of every catch.
- **Site Nine.** A Halcyon drilling platform over a deep sill in the open Girdle, guarded and unlisted. Beneath it, under silt, lies an entire drowned Maker city, intact, with dry halls for a million and wet halls for a million more. In its lowest vault, behind three doors, is the Makers' engine: a ring forty metres across set flush in the floor, and a lance that can be pointed.

### The Bright Shelves (south Shoulder)

*Shallow, clear, sunlit water over white sand, as far as the eye can see. Seagrass, glass coral, shellfish. Holds here break the surface. The danger is that there is nowhere to hide.*

- **Nine Spires.** The largest hold in the world, fifty thousand people in nine fluted white Maker towers that rise from the seabed through the surface and thirty metres into the air, with stone aprons at the tide line where Driftfolk rafts tie up. Mariners here trade openly with Driftfolk at the tide line and are the worldliest of their people. Northern holds think them half agravar.
- **Pearl Stair.** Terraces of shellfish beds stepping down a long slope, worked by a dozen small holds that share one council. Famous for sea-silk, pearls and stubbornness.
- **Tidehall.** A drowned Maker amphitheatre, a bowl a kilometre across with perfect sound, empty and unclaimed. The southern Moots meet here. It could seat every speaker of every hold, and never has.

### The Quiet (south Tip)

*Dark, warm water above volcanic vents. The surface steams. It is silent in a way that feels watched. The danger is heat, poison gas, and the rays' judgement.*

- **Ember House.** Jef and Maelis's south home, on a black-sand islet with hot springs. Much like Kettle House, with a still-room full of Maelis's jars instead of a workshop.
- **The Smokers.** Fields of volcanic chimneys pouring black, mineral-rich water. Red tube-gardens grow as thick as forest. The vent growths in Maelis's potions come from here.
- **The Wheel.** Open dark water where the great rays gather, turning slowly in a ring kilometres wide, as they have since before the Makers. No hold has ever been built within sight of it.
- **The Hush.** The southern hospital, at the bottom of the Well: unlike the Lantern it is not Maker work but was built by mariner hands, block on block. It is unlit, built of black stone, warm, and completely silent. Healers and patients do not speak inside. It treats the sicknesses of the mind as well as the body.

## 13. Conflicts and secrets

**Open conflicts**

- Corporation against corporation, for islands.
- Corporations against the sea, through dumping and deep drilling.
- Tenders against Breakers inside every hold.
- The Threaded against agravar intrusion, with fewer Threaded each year.

**Secrets for the game master**

1. **Jef is dying and has no successor.** When he goes, nobody can repair the holds, the Dragon or the Wells, and nobody can make new Threaded.
2. **The holds are failing.** Lights, air and heat are going out in the oldest, Deep Chorus among them, and the elders are hiding it.
3. **Halcyon has found the drowned city under Site Nine,** and in it a Maker engine that can boil a sea. Kessler-Vane is building a hull to carry the part that can be pointed, the lance. The larger part, the ring, has never left the vault, and Meridian has kept it for itself.
4. **Brightwater holds a captured Wellwalker** in a clinic tower in Saltgate, trying to make intended water last.
5. **Breakers are learning to work in chorus,** with a city in mind.
6. **Agravars and mariners are one people.** The Makers were the ancestors of both. The drowned city's archive walls still show it.
7. **The gift and the craft can meet in one person.** It is about to happen for the first time. See the campaign.
8. **Meridian's chairman is Jef's lost apprentice.** Anselm Rook, about seventy-two, was the only survivor of a ferry lost in the Belt Storms when he was fourteen. The rays carried him to the Hush. Jef found him sweeping it, took him north as his fifty-ninth apprentice and gave him the Thread forty-nine years ago, hoping that hearing the machines would teach his hands. It did not: in eight years he never opened a seam. He learned everything else: where the holds lie, that they are failing, and that there was once a city under the Girdle. He wanted Jef to build for the islands so that nobody need fear for ground again; Jef, sworn never to build, refused without saying why. Rook took his needle and went up. He still carries the Thread and wears a high collar over the scars. He keeps the mariners half myth on purpose, and his paper keeps the five boards from each other's throats. He has no gift and cannot mend. He is courteous, patient, never raises his voice, never pretends, and is right about several things.
9. **What Rook wants.** Ground. Run at the scale of the city, with a heart large enough to drive it, the engine's ring can hold the sea off the whole Girdle shelf: two hundred thousand square kilometres at sixty metres or less. The water it holds back spreads over every ocean on Ovara and raises them by the width of two fingers. A wide ring of deaf water forms around the dry ground and would take the Anthem Waters as far north as Chorus Canyon and the near half of the Bright Shelves. He has tanks ready for the holds. He needs two things he cannot make: hands that open Maker work, and the Dragon's heart. The Meridian Scholarship was his search for the hands.
10. **The engine is not a weapon and not a wall.** The Makers built the ring when the sea began to rise, to lift their city with the water a little at a time, so that its top was always dry and its foot always wet. Seated wrong and run by force it orders the sea away instead, in jumps, and the water it orders goes deaf. If it stops or fails, the standing wall falls in one piece. One faction of Makers ran it that way; a sea died and the wall fell. They shut it behind three doors, and those who had done it and those who had not parted on a shore. That quarrel is the true beginning of the two peoples.
11. **There were hundreds of cities.** The Dragon ran between them. Jef only ever looked for the greatest. The rays remember where all of them lie.
12. **What the rays want.** They have carried everyone's memory alone since before the Makers. They want the archive walls lit, so that they can put some of it down.

## 14. Running games here

**Tone.** Cyberpunk on the surface, mythic and quiet below, with the tension coming from contact between the two.

| Background | Hook |
|---|---|
| Mariner Tidecaller | Powerful, but weaker every year the water worsens |
| Mariner healer or Wellwalker | Hunted for what they can do |
| Threaded | Unmatched pilot bound by a vow |
| Agravar corporate defector | Knows what is being dumped and where |
| Driftfolk trader or smuggler | Belongs nowhere, welcome almost everywhere |
| Shallowborn go-between | Half of each world, fully of neither |
| Wrecker | Knows the ruins; owes money to the wrong people |

**Using the gift at the table.** Whatever rules you play with, three questions settle most scenes: what is the character's reach, what state is the water in, and are they ebbed? Reaching one step beyond your reach is possible once, and invites the undertow.

**Starting adventures outside the campaign**

1. A dead Wellwalker drifts into a hold with an agravar bullet in them and a Dragon token in their hand.
2. A Brightwater tanker sinks over a kelp farm. The crew is alive inside, the cargo is leaking, and the hold is arguing over whether to save them.
3. Something fouled comes out of the Slick and into the terraces of Low Anthem.
4. At Saltgate, a Kessler-Vane engineer offers a fortune for one hour with a Threaded's vehicle.

## 15. Decisions made

1. **The gift is true magic,** from the sea, and cannot be copied by technology.
2. **Tidecallers are a minority:** about one mariner in ten is touched, with roughly one Mighty per hold.
3. **Agravars can receive the Thread, rarely.** They can never have the gift. Shallowborn rarely inherit it.
4. **The great rays are a third intelligent people,** and the Queen is their voice.
5. **Ordinary agravars think mariners are half myth,** and the corporations keep it that way.
6. **Jef and the Queen live together,** with a home at each Tip, travelling between them on no fixed schedule.
7. **The sea can be ordered, and it kills the water that obeys.** Ordered water can be restored, but only slowly and by many.
8. **There is one central antagonist,** Anselm Rook of Meridian, who is neither a caller nor a mechanic.
