# Ingestion conflicts

Places where sources, or a source and the world rules in `CLAUDE.md`, seem to disagree. Each records both versions and what canon now says.

## power.ordering: ordering before chapter 11

- **CLAUDE.md, world rule 9:** "Nobody orders the sea before chapter 11. The hero learns how in chapter 10."
- **campaign.md#chapter-10:** Rook sets the engine's governor in a bowl of sea and drives it through the Thread; the water goes deaf for good. **world-bible.md#6** says the Makers' great engine orders the same way, and **world-bible.md#13** that it orders the sea when run by force.
- **Reading applied:** no person orders the sea before chapter 11; in chapter 10 the engine does, on a bowl, and Rook has no gift (world rule 10). `power.ordering` records the chapter 10 bowl as the engine's work. Owner to confirm that rule 9 means people, not the engine.

## power.reach-unheard-of: nobody at reach 5

- **world-bible.md#6:** reach 5 is held by "Nobody, yet".
- **campaign.md (what the game master knows):** the hero's reach is 5, which nobody has had before.
- **Reading applied:** no conflict; "yet" leaves room for the hero. Both facts are recorded.

## power.restore, wells-water: where Restore works

- **world-bible.md#6 (the four forms):** Restore is "only reliably possible at the Wells"; at the Hush it is used on minds.
- **world-bible.md#6 (two kinds of deaf) and campaign.md#chapter-17:** the healers of the Hush restore ordered water in the open sea, six thousand of them, a hand's width at a time.
- **Chosen:** the later, specific passage (many healers, slowly, with the living sea leaning in). "Reliably" leaves room for the slow, many-handed case. Order of authority: both are world bible, so the specific one wins over the general.

## place.the-hush: who built it

- **world-bible.md#3:** the Makers built the undersea cities, the Dragon and "the buildings in the Wells".
- **world-bible.md#11:** the Hush, at the bottom of the south Well, "is not Maker work but was built by mariner hands".
- **Chosen:** the Hush is mariner-built; the Lantern is Maker work. The history table is read as naming Maker buildings in the Wells, not all of them. The glossary says "the Wells' buildings" for the Makers; read it with this exception.

## machine.the-thread: who can carry it

- **world-bible.md#7:** "Any willing body can take the Thread, agravar included."
- **world-bible.md#15 (decision 3):** "Agravars can receive the Thread, rarely."
- **Chosen:** §15 outranks §7, but "rarely" is read as how often it happens, not whether it can. This agrees with world rule 3 in `CLAUDE.md`.

## machine.the-dragon: it never stops

- **world-bible.md#8:** the Dragon circles "without ever stopping"; at each Tip it rounds the Well at walking pace.
- **campaign.md#chapter-3:** when the Dragon comes to Greywatch Stone "it stops for him, which it has never done for anyone."
- **Chosen:** the bible for the Dragon's normal behaviour; the campaign's stop is the event of chapter 3, recorded as a change on the entry. It stalls for good in chapter 5.

## person.hesper: her reach

- **campaign.md (companions table):** the Mighty of Low Anthem, calling, reach 3.
- **campaign.md#chapter-2:** "on her best day can stop a fishing boat". The bible gives that as reach 2 ("a boat's worth"); reach 3 stops a vessel.
- **Chosen:** reach 3, from the bible's definition of the Mighty. The chapter 2 line is a best day, not her limit. The novel, which names her, is not contradicted.

## person.anselm-rook: his age

- **world-bible.md#13 and campaign.md (the villain):** about seventy-two.
- **campaign.md#chapter-4:** "an islander of seventy". The novel says "perhaps seventy", as a first impression.
- **Chosen:** seventy-two. The other two are what the hero sees, not a stated age.

## person.sefa: when she joins

- **campaign.md (companions table):** "travels with him from chapter 6".
- **campaign.md#chapter-5:** Sefa acts as a companion throughout (slaps him out of the undertow; "Sefa's moment"). Chapter 6 then says she "joins the journey".
- **Chosen:** present from chapter 5 as the story needs her; formal travel from chapter 6. See `gaps.md`, chapters.

## place.kettle-house and place.ember-house: the still-room

- **world-bible.md#11:** Ember House has "a still-room full of Maelis's jars instead of a workshop"; Kettle House has a workshop.
- **novel (Kettle House, line 925 of the source):** Maelis goes into "the still-room, where she kept her jars"; **campaign.md#chapter-4** says the same ("behind the still-room door"). The novel later calls Ember House "the twin of Kettle House" with the still-room where the workshop was.
- **Chosen:** the bible: the still-room is at Ember House. Content set at Kettle House says "behind a door" and not "still-room" unless the owner decides otherwise. Needs the owner only if a scene turns on it.

## event.the-long-way-round: engineers who follow Ilan

- **campaign.md#chapter-9 and #chapter-13/17:** three hundred and eleven engineers follow him; "three hundred" later.
- **novel (the speech at Chorus, line 2849 of the source):** forty from Keelhaven and "two hundred and seventy more at Lashing", 310.
- **Chosen:** the campaign's 311 as the count at chapter 9; later "three hundred" is rounding. Names and speech from the novel.

## faction.threaded: how many

- **world-bible.md#8:** about a hundred today.
- **campaign.md#chapter-6:** forty-one in the gallery, fourteen leave with Rook, and the chapter later says "nineteen needles left" and "twenty-seven Threaded" at the heart; chapters 14 to 17 give fifteen returning and twenty-two with Rook.
- **Chosen:** the bible's hundred as the order's size; the campaign's figures are who is in the gallery or on a side at that moment. The campaign's own counts do not add up exactly (41 less 14 is 27, not 19). Content must use only the figures of its own chapter and name no total.
