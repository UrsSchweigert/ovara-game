# Writing guide

One page. Canon decides what is true; this decides how it sounds.

## The panel

- A dialogue panel holds **three short lines**, each about **40 characters**.
- One idea to a panel. Plain, concrete words. No narrator, no commentary.
- Use canon terms only (`canon/glossary.yaml`). Never use a banned word.
- Names and facts come from canon by ID. The samples below are voice, not story.
- Sample blocks are separated by blank lines; each block is one panel.

## Mariners

Slow, patient, in images of water and tending. They speak of
mending, tides and lamps, and say little about themselves.

```
The hold keeps what the tide brings in.

Ask the sea. Do not order it.

Mend the net before the quarrel.

Sit. The elders speak last, and slowly.

Lampweed fades. We hang more.
```

## Agravars

Fast, clipped, in ownership, numbers and brand names. Everything
has a price, a deadline or an owner.

```
That berth is leased. Forty scrip a day.

Your pass lapsed at nine. Step back.

Everything here is owned. Even the air.

Dump it in the sea. It's cheaper.

Meridian pays on time. Remember that.
```

## Driftfolk

Agravars with no corporation. Dry, wary, weather-wise; they trade
with both sides and trust neither. They read the Eyes and the gale skates.

```
Gale skates are low. Storm by dusk.

Raft-town leaves at the next Eye.

I carry what the corporations won't.

Fair price, and no questions. Both ways.

Tie up quick. Lashing doesn't wait.
```

## The hero

Short, honest, never joking about what he did. Before chapter 7 he is
called Wrack and has no past, so he asks more than he states.

```
I don't know my name.

Tell me what I did.

I asked. It answered. It shouldn't have.

I won't order the sea again.

Somebody had better send word north.
```

## Anselm Rook

Courteous and patient. He never raises his voice and never pretends.
Long calm sentences cut to fit the panel; he is right about several things.

```
Sit. I will not keep you long.

Forty million islanders need ground.

I never lied to you. I only waited.

I aimed at a machine. He went down.

I should like to see it run.
```

## The great rays

The rays use no words and have **no text panel**. What they show is a
picture panel with no words (`docs/style/art-direction.md`). Describe
the picture in the scene script, never as speech:

- A ship-sized shadow turns slowly over the Wheel.
- The hero's own hands, small, over a dark floor, as the undertow takes.
- A far hold, lamps lit, seen from above the water.

Do not caption these. Do not give a ray a voice. Queen Maelis is the
one person they speak through, and she speaks in her own words.
